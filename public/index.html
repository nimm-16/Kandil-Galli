Big and custom kandils

More than a lantern. Generations of craft.

Kandil Galli is a narrow lane in Mahim West, near Mahim station, where families have made akash kandils by hand since at least the late 1940s. For a few weeks every year the whole lane glows.

1940sKandils made in the lane since at least then
20 to 40Artisan families, mostly Marathi
2 to 3Generations of making, in the households we met
100 to 150Lanterns made per home each season

The lane

Reports name two clusters in the lane, Kavle Wadi and Kadri Wadi. One account traces the craft to nineteenth-century Eid lanterns; today the lane sits near Citylight Market and St Michael’s Church and sells for Diwali, Christmas and other festivals.

Most makers work in the evenings, after physically demanding day jobs. Materials are bought in bulk from mid to end September, and every season they try to make as many colours, sizes and new designs as they can. Prices are agreed together, because undercutting a neighbour, in the makers’ words, “would lead to differences in the community”.

How a kandil is made

The craft core the makers protect: hand-cut paper and fabric, hand assembly, tassels and family designs.

  1. Materials, bought togetherPaper, fabric and trims are sourced in bulk in late September to keep costs down.
  2. A reusable frameMany households have moved from bamboo to reusable frames, saving time every season.
  3. Hand-cuttingPatterns are cut by hand into paper or fabric. No printed imitations.
  4. Hand assemblyPanels are pasted and fitted to the frame, one face at a time.
  5. Tassels and tailsLong paper tails and tassels finish the traditional shapes.
  6. Lit in the laneFinished kandils hang outside the homes where they were made.

Our artisans

Makers are not named here. Names, photographs and family stories will be added only with each household’s consent.

Why buying handmade matters

Plastic and machine-made lanterns win on looks that change every year and on being sold everywhere. The lane competes on something they can’t copy: hand skill passed down in families. When you buy here, the customer and the income stay with the makers. There is no commission on this marketplace.

What staysHand-cut paper and fabric, tassels, family designs, traditional shapes, hand assembly, reusable frames.
What can changeColour palettes and finishes, standard frame sizes, folding frames for shipping, ready-made LED fittings.
What the lane avoidsPrinted imitations of hand-cut work, single-use or thermocol frames, factory production.
